I've been meaning to build a proper personal site for years. The problem is that I'm a chronic
procrastinator, and a personal site is exactly the kind of project that loses every fight for my
attention. What finally got it over the line was AI. The productivity boost turned out to be just
barely enough to drag me past the activation energy, so here we are.

The one rule I set for myself: don't build another boring CV site. There are plenty of those already.
I wanted something that isn't lame and is actually fun to land on, more like a place you step into
than a list of links with my name at the top.

## The idea

The concept was simple. What if your projects lived in a space you could actually explore? Not
thumbnails in a grid, but things that float and react when you get close. I kept coming back to the
image of a star chart, where every project is its own body with its own gravity.

I also wanted the whole thing to be self-referential. The site is the first exhibit in its own
gallery. The "This Site" node you clicked to get here is the thing you're reading about right now.

## What I actually built

The main view is a React Three Fiber canvas, a WebGL scene that fills the entire screen. Each project
renders as a glowing sphere that distorts organically, a "body" in the cosmos metaphor. The
background is a custom GLSL shader: an animated nebula made from layered simplex noise. A drifting
starfield sits on top, with a few brighter stars sweeping in close to sell the depth.

Clicking a body navigates to its page using the View Transitions API, which morphs the sphere from
its spot in 3D into the page header. That morph is one of my favourite details on the whole site.

## What I'm still figuring out

The first real test of "is this any good" was opening the site on a slower laptop, where it ran like
a slideshow. That turned into a whole performance pass: the scene now measures its own frame rate and
quietly scales detail up or down to match the machine. Strong computer, full glory. Weak computer,
still smooth. There's a writeup of how that works in the deep dive.

The bigger open question is content. The cosmos has exactly one real project right now (itself), which
is a little circular. As I build and ship more things, they show up as new bodies. The plan is a
gallery that grows with the work, not a launch-day museum that never changes.

I also want live demos running inside the project nodes. The architecture already allows it, but
making a demo that's actually worth showing takes real time.
